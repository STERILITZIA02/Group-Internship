## 2. Working time and attendance

We have to work five days a week, from Monday to Friday, with an expected commitment of eight hours per day and 40 hours per week for each member.
Our standard working hours are set from 9:30 am to 5:30 pm, with 10-minute morning and afternoon tea breaks, and are flexible for each team member as needed. Evenings and weekends are normally reserved for personal time (if an urgent situation arises, this might change).

This is our meeting schedule, including the estimated time and frequency for each type of meeting.

| Meeting | Frequency | Time / Duration | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daily Stand-up | Every development day | 9:15 am, 10–15 min | Share progress, daily plans and blockers |
| Sprint Planning | First Monday of each sprint | 45–60 min | Define sprint goals, estimate effort and allocate tasks |
| Weekly Progress Review | Every Friday, unless we need do a Sprint Presentation | 30 min | Review progress, workload and upcoming tasks |
| Sprint Presentation | Last Friday of each sprint | 30–60 min | Demonstrate completed work, reflect and identify improvements for the last two weeks |
| Technical Discussion | As needed | Flexible | Discuss technical decisions and resolve blockers |

If someone expects to be late or away, they need to let the group know as soon as possible and the reason why. Every meeting will be recorded in audio. A member who misses a meeting can catch up from the notes and recorded audio.

## 3. Task agreements and workload

In the GitHub Issue or shared task list, record the expected result, owner, target date and how the team will check the work. Add an estimate, reviewer or dependency where it helps. We divide work according to availability, skills and learning goals. Research, testing, writing, coordination and helping a teammate count alongside development.

## 4. Communication and records

Our team will mainly use face-to-face conversation and WeChat as the messaging channel. If there is an urgent decision,

Check the team channel on working days and aim to acknowledge messages by the next working day. For an urgent decision, say what is blocked and when an answer is needed. Contact the PM directly if the message has not reached the people involved.

Keep meeting actions and decisions with the relevant issue or Wiki entry. Each member adds their own work and reflection to the weekly statement.

## 5. Collaboration and quality

Keep shared work in the repository. Review code in pull requests, check the result against the task and tell the next person what still needs doing. Credit each contributor and the sources or assistance used.

## 6. Decisions and disagreements

Task owners make routine implementation decisions. Discuss scope, deadlines and changes that affect someone else's work with the group. Hear the people involved. If agreement stalls, use a simple majority after all five members have had a chance to comment. The PM records the decision and updates the plan.

Raise a disagreement privately with the people involved and agree on a next step. The PM can facilitate, or another member if the PM is involved. Ask the supervisor for help if the group cannot resolve it.

## 7. Problems and crisis management

**Absence or loss of contact.** After an unexplained absence or missed update, a teammate checks in privately that working day. Allow until the next working day for a reply. If two working days pass and shared work is affected, the PM or an available teammate contacts the supervisor. Contact staff sooner if a deadline needs an immediate decision. On return, agree on a manageable workload and temporary cover where needed.

**Work running late.** Tell the group what remains. Agree on help, a smaller task or a new internal date. If the deadline is within two working days, discuss it that day where practical. The PM asks course staff about options when a submission is at risk.

**Uneven workload or repeated missed commitments.** Speak privately, agree on support or a smaller task and check back at the next meeting. If the problem persists and affects others, discuss it together and seek staff advice if needed.

**Technical blocker.** Share the failure, what you tried and which task is held up. Pair up or switch to work that can continue. At the next check-in, choose a workaround or ask for help. Record who will act and when the team will check back.
