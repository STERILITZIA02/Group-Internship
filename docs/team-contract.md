## 2. Working time and attendance

We will plan around the course timetable and agree on shared availability at the start of each week. The lecture uses a 40-hour weekday schedule as a reference; we will confirm the course's required hours and how breaks are counted before filling in our weekly commitments.

Individual start and finish times can vary around agreed meetings and course sessions. Evenings and weekends are normally personal time. If someone expects to be away or late, they will let the group know as soon as practical and share any update needed by others.

We propose a 20-30 minute planning meeting early in the week and a short progress review near the end. The team may move or combine these meetings when the course schedule makes that easier. Anyone who misses a meeting can contribute in writing and catch up from the notes.

## 3. Task agreements and workload

At weekly planning, we will divide work according to availability, skills, and learning goals. Each task will have an owner, an expected result, a target date, and a way to check completion. A second person can help or review where useful. Larger tasks can be split into smaller pieces.

The owner will give a short update at the next check-in. If a task is taking longer than expected, they will explain what remains and suggest a new date, a smaller scope, or help from another member. Changes can be agreed in the team channel and recorded with the task.

We will review the workload each week. Research, testing, documentation, coordination, and helping others count as contributions alongside implementation. The aim is a fair contribution across the project; everyone's task count or hours need not match each week.

## 4. Communication and records

We will use one agreed team channel for routine discussion and GitHub for tasks, code, and shared documents. Check the team channel on working days and aim to acknowledge messages by the next working day. A short reply is enough when a full answer needs more time.

For a time-sensitive problem, state what is blocked and when a response is needed. Contact the PM directly if the group message has not reached the people needed. Replies outside agreed working time are optional unless someone has arranged otherwise.

Keep a short note of decisions, task changes, and meeting actions in the Wiki or the related Issue. Each person will add their contribution and reflection to the weekly statement before the team reviews it. Record actual work and any hours required by the course.

## 5. Collaboration and quality

Keep shared work in the repository so another member can pick it up. Ask for help when blocked and explain unfinished work when handing it over. Use pull requests and the repository's review process for code changes. Check work against the task's completion criteria before calling it done.

We share responsibility for the project outcome and will credit each person's work. Shared responsibility does not change the repository's existing ownership or licence arrangements. Sources and any assistance will be acknowledged as the course requires.

## 6. Decisions and disagreements

The person doing a task can make routine implementation decisions within its agreed scope. Changes affecting other members, the project scope, or a course deadline will be discussed with the group.

For a group decision, hear the affected members' views and try to agree on a practical option. If discussion stalls, use a simple majority after all five members have had a reasonable chance to comment. The PM records the decision and any follow-up. We can revisit it when new evidence appears.

Raise disagreements with the people involved and describe the specific work or behaviour causing difficulty. Give each person time to explain, then agree on a next step. The PM can help the discussion; if the PM is involved, another member can facilitate. Ask the supervisor or course staff for help if the group cannot reach a workable agreement.

## 7. Problems and crisis management

### Absence or loss of contact

If someone misses an agreed meeting or update without notice, the PM or a teammate will send a private check-in that working day. Allow until the next working day for a reply. If there is still no response after two working days and shared work is affected, Junan will contact the supervisor or course staff for advice. Another member can do this if Junan is unavailable. Contact staff sooner if a course deadline needs an immediate decision.

Once contact resumes, discuss availability and agree on a manageable return to work. For illness or personal difficulties, the member only needs to share what the team needs for planning. Urgent tasks can be covered temporarily and the workload reviewed at the next check-in.

### A task or deadline is slipping

Tell the group when a commitment is at risk. Identify the remaining work and decide whether to add help, reduce the task, or change the internal date. For a deadline within the next two working days, hold a brief discussion that day where practical. The PM will ask course staff about options if a course deadline is affected; the team cannot grant its own extension.

### Repeated missed commitments or uneven workload

Start with a private conversation about the cause. Agree on a smaller task, support, or a revised date and review it at the next check-in. If the same problem continues and affects others, discuss it as a group and then seek staff advice if needed. Keep a factual record of agreed actions and contributions.

### Technical blockers or unavailable tools

Share what failed, what has been tried, and which tasks depend on it. Pair up for a short investigation or move to work that can continue. If the blocker remains by the next check-in, agree on a simpler alternative or ask for technical guidance. Update the plan if the proposed solution changes.

For any problem that changes the plan, record the agreed action, its owner, and the next check-in date. Review whether the action helped before adding more steps.
